[Place and date]

Political chatbots study: "Vaalikeskustelu" at the public lecture "Kohti älykkyyttä, ja sen ohi!"

## 1. The study

You are invited to take part because you are attending the public lecture "Kohti älykkyyttä, ja sen ohi!" at the Metso main library (Lehmus hall, Pirkankatu 2, Tampere) on 23 October 2026. The study is part of the SYNTHETICA project ("Multifaceted ripple effects and limitations of human-AI interplay at work, business and society"), funded by the Research Council of Finland and led by Professor Pekka Abrahamsson (Tampere University), with the University of Vaasa and the University of Jyväskylä as partners.

We study how people experience conversations with AI chatbots about Finnish politics: the chatbot's use of language, how it presents political issues, and its possible means of persuasion. In particular, we want to know whether people notice when a chatbot has a political slant. The chatbot was built by the research team for this study. It is not a service of any political party.

## 2. Voluntariness

Taking part is voluntary. Nobody checks whether you scan the code, and you can follow the whole lecture whether or not you take part. You can stop at any time by closing the page, without giving a reason and without consequences. You may leave any survey question unanswered.

You take part without giving your name. We cannot connect you to your conversation afterwards unless you give us your session code, shown on the chat page and at the end. If you close the page during the conversation, the messages sent so far remain in the research data, which do not contain your name. At the end you can delete your own data with one button, or keep the session code and ask us later to delete the session. Without the code we cannot find your data. Once results have been published in summarised form, deletion removes your data from the dataset but cannot change what has been published.

## 3. Progress of the study

1. You scan the QR code with your own phone. The page opens in your browser in Finnish or English. No app is installed and no account is created.
2. You read the summary on the consent page and confirm that you wish to take part and that you are 18 or older. This notification and the privacy notice are linked from that page.
3. You chat with the chatbot about Finnish politics for as long as you like, typically 10-15 minutes [confirm]. You can ask about any topic you like. You do not need to reveal your own opinions: you can ask neutral questions or play the role of any party's supporter. The chatbot may be designed to express a particular political viewpoint. We do not tell you in advance whether this is the case for your conversation or which viewpoint it would be, because the study asks whether people notice this on their own.
4. You answer a short survey (about 3 minutes) about your use of chatbots and your experience of the conversation.
5. On the final page we explain how the chatbot was set up for your conversation and what the study is about. You also get a session code and a button to delete your data.

Please do not write your name, contact details, or other details you would not want stored. The research data (your messages, the chatbot's replies, your survey answers, the time, the language and the setting given to the chatbot) are stored on servers of CSC, the Finnish national research IT centre, in Finland. Only the research team has access to them. If you use the microphone, your recording is sent to the study's own server at CSC in Finland only to turn it into text, and it is not stored; the text is stored like a typed message. The chatbot's replies are read aloud by the study's own speech server. We do not collect your name, email address, phone number, IP address or party preference. We do not ask your age, gender or occupation.

## 4. Possible benefits of the study

You will not benefit directly. The study helps us understand how AI chatbots may influence political conversation, how well people recognise slant and persuasion, and what safeguards democratic debate needs as chatbots become common sources of political information. The results are published in scientific venues and used in teaching. You may find the explanation on the final page useful for your own use of chatbots.

## 5. Possible risks, harm and inconvenience, and how we prepare for them

The study involves no physical procedures. The conversation concerns political topics at the level of public debate, takes place on your own phone, and can be ended at any time.

Three points deserve attention:
- The chatbot may present political issues from one viewpoint and may use persuasive language. Its statements are generated by an AI model and may be inaccurate. They are not statements by any party. At the end we tell you how the chatbot was set up, so that you do not leave with a false impression.
- If you write your own political opinions in the chat or the survey, they become part of the research data. Political opinions are sensitive data under EU law. You are free to keep them to yourself; the study does not need them.
- Where and by whom your messages are processed to generate the replies is described in section 3. This is why we ask you not to type identifying details.

Stopping participation and deleting your data are described in section 2.

## 6. Costs and compensation, funding

Taking part is free of charge and no compensation is paid. You use your own phone and your own mobile data or the library's wireless network. The study is funded by the Research Council of Finland through the SYNTHETICA project.
